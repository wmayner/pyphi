The argument that picks the units of the candidate system is now `subset` everywhere, and it is keyword-only, as on `pyphi.analyze`: `Substrate.sia(state, subset=...)` and `Substrate.ces(...)` replace `indices`, `System(substrate, state, subset=...)` replaces the `node_indices` argument (the resolved units are still `system.node_indices`), and `actual.events`, `actual.true_events` and `actual.extrinsic_events` take `subset` in place of `nodes` and `indices`. `System.from_substrate` is removed; call `System(...)`. `copy.replace(system, state=...)` keeps the system's units; `dataclasses.replace` needs `subset=system.node_indices`, as its error says. The analysis methods `Substrate.sia`, `ces`, `complexes`, `maximal_complex`, `irreducible_sias` and `all_sias`, and `System.sia`, `ces`, `distinctions`, `mic`, `mie` and `phi`, now raise `TypeError` on a keyword argument they do not use, where they used to ignore it: `substrate.sia(state, subset=(0, 1))` analyzed the whole substrate, and `substrate.complexes(state, formalism="IIT_3_0")` returned IIT 4.0 complexes. The same check applies to `pyphi.formalism.iit4.sia` and `pyphi.formalism.iit3.sia`. The `Substrate` methods, `System.sia` and `System.ces` accept `formalism=`, with the same meaning as on `analyze`, and the complex-search methods also take `parallel=` and `progress=`. `System.distinctions(congruent=...)` is keyword-only. `pyphi.conf.presets.use(formalism)` returns the context manager that applies a formalism's preset.
